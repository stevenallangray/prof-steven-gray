## People

### Principal Investigator
**Dr. Steven Gray** — Principal Investigator
I'm an environmental social scientist interested in how people understand complex environmental problems, how different kinds of knowledge come together, and how groups make decisions about the future. Much of my research explores collective intelligence, knowledge diversity, and systems thinking, particularly how we can draw on what people know, individually and collectively, to make better decisions about social and environmental change.
My work combines participatory research with computational modeling and often involves working directly with communities, scientists, and decision-makers. Over the years, I've worked on a wide range of environmental challenges, including fisheries management, biodiversity conservation, climate adaptation, coastal resilience, and sustainable food systems. I'm particularly interested in developing approaches that help us understand not only what people know, but also how differences in knowledge influence collective decisions and their outcomes.
I'm also the developer of [Mental Modeler](https://www.mentalmodeler.com), a freely available tool for mapping and analyzing how people understand complex systems. It's been rewarding to see the software adopted by researchers, educators, and practitioners across many disciplines.
I'm currently a Professor in the Department of Community Sustainability at Michigan State University and a Research Scientist at Johns Hopkins University's Whiting School of Engineering, where my work is funded through the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). I've previously held academic and research appointments in Hawaii, Massachusetts, Copenhagen, and Berlin. Much of what I enjoy about academic research comes from collaborating with people across disciplines and places, and from finding new ways to connect scientific understanding with practical environmental decisions.

#### Abigail Ording — MS Student
Abby is a master's student in the Department of Community Sustainability at Michigan State University. Her research examines relationships among culture, the biophysical characteristics of nature, and human well-being. She is particularly interested in how environmental design influences ecosystem services and public health outcomes. Her recent research includes studies of urban greenspace and mental health in Flint, Michigan, and relationships between urban biodiversity and maternal health. Abby holds a BS in Environmental Studies and Sustainability from MSU and plans to pursue doctoral study.

![Irene Luján Climent portrait](img/people/irene-lujan-climent.webp)
#### Irene Luján Climent — Visiting PhD Researcher
Irene is a PhD candidate in Political Science at the Universitat de València, a member of the OVAGAP research group, and a Fulbright Predoctoral Research Fellow at Michigan State University. Her research examines governance and management of the commons, focusing on how institutional arrangements influence collective decision-making and shared-resource management. Through the BlueGreen Governance project (Horizon Europe/UKRI), she studies coastal wetland governance in the Valencian Region using multilevel governance and ecofeminist perspectives. At MSU, she is developing a comparative study of water governance in the Great Lakes region and Europe.
